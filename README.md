# Tranzit

A long-form transport and business simulation built in Unity.

The player starts as a small regional carrier and can grow into a multinational transport group while cities, industries, infrastructure and technology evolve around them.

## First playable V1

The first implementation target is a genuinely playable offline Windows game, not a technology demonstration. Its approved scope is:

- one new-game preset: **1900**, with continuing calendar and technological progression;
- **rail and road**, for freight and passengers, including intercity and local buses;
- real geography covering the territory of present-day Czechia plus adjoining parts of Germany, Poland, Austria and Slovakia, with an authored historically plausible 1900 world;
- existing infrastructure, physical vehicles/cargo, real AI competitors and the applicable connected business/operations mechanics;
- cohesive stylized 3D model-world graphics, complete **Czech and English** UI and full save/load;
- one accounting unit named **money**, shown in compact UI amounts with a dedicated neutral coin/token icon rather than a real-world currency symbol;
- historical vehicle models without artificial end-year removal; finite market offers and changing maintenance support remain economic constraints.

The detailed boundary and cross-system rules are in [V1 Scope](docs/V1_SCOPE.md). Water, tram, trolleybus and metro operation and the later start presets remain wider base-game goals rather than requirements of this first release.

**Confirmed:** a restrained contemporary dark interface, movable/resizable floating management/detail windows, multiple views, reusable ordinary selection details with content pinning, explicit opening in another window, minimize/restore, remembered/recoverable layout and a fixed bottom navigation/status/time bar. The top-level navigation is Build, Operations, Business, Assets, Company and World. The bottom bar replaces mandatory permanent left/top strips; contextual construction tools do not remove it. Ordinary object clicks reuse an unpinned detail rather than opening a new window every time. Pinning retains the object's identity while its data stays live.

**Global minimalism, tooltips and consistency:** every screen follows a concise visible overview, supporting explanations on hover/keyboard focus, and click-open full details. Group functions by the player's task and reuse one visual/interaction system for windows, typography, spacing, cards, tables, controls, links and semantic states. Keep important status, blockers, units/periods, primary actions and required cost/impact disclosures visible; do not hide essential information or the agreed vehicle/Line lists inside tooltips. Ordinary first-layer tooltips are required in V1 with an accessible non-hover path and no gameplay side effects; deep nested glossary chains remain deferred. The station board retains its approved character with consistent window controls. Section 2.1 / UI-D15 owns these rules. Exact design tokens and unspecified screen layouts remain design work, not permission to disregard the global principles.

**Connected windows and inline object links:** references to specific vehicles, stations, Lines, Trips, contracts and other inspectable objects are directly clickable in messages, detail windows, tables and planners. A vehicle mention opens that exact vehicle, not a fleet search or catalogue model. Reuse/focus existing windows, preserve pinned identities and drafts, and keep a path back to the source. Links use stable identities, work with keyboard and mouse in CZ/EN, and handle historical/unavailable targets without guessing a replacement. Navigation does not issue an order, jump the camera or resume a pause. Section 4.1 / UI-D09 owns the full rule; this does not make future deep nested tooltips mandatory.

**Vehicle detail:** the confirmed default is a compact operational overview with current activity/problem, location, applicable load/destination and next task, plus a small preview of the correct vehicle model. A static model image is sufficient; no live camera feed or animated 3D viewer is required. Actual vehicle information remains live and separate from the thumbnail. Detailed technical parameters, costs and history stay in secondary views; exact tab labels/dimensions remain design work. See Section 5.2 / UI-D10.

**Fleet and acquisition:** Assets / Vehicles opens Fleet, Vehicle Market and Orders and deliveries in one workspace. Actual vehicle rows show location, current/next use and time-specific availability; idle is not necessarily free. Model browsing compares real manufacturer, dealer-stock, used and lease offers, with no fabricated inventory or model end-year ban. Contextual market filters retain the source requirement and distinguish technical fit from timely delivery. Explicit review separates vehicle terms, immediate/later payments and included, quoted or unarranged transport. Partial orders track real units through production, transit, receipt and operational readiness using existing delivery systems. [UI_FLEET.md](docs/UI_FLEET.md) owns UI-D22 and its acceptance scenarios; it complements the existing vehicle detail and Line roster without new market or allocation mechanics.

**Line planning:** use independently openable cards, not a guided wizard. The player can edit in any order, save an incomplete Line/Pattern design, close it and continue in a later session. Unlaunched plans remain visible separately from operating services. The overview shows actual dependencies and readiness, distinguishing filled inputs, forecasts, pending work and secured rights/resources. Saving a draft does not operate a service, reserve resources or create sales/Trips; separately accepted purchases and agreements still have real costs. Launch is an explicit, freshly validated commitment, never a consequence of filling every card or reaching an intended start date. Active Pattern edits still use future versions and existing impact checks. Section 6.1 / UI-D11 owns the full rule, including save/load, stale readiness and activation safeguards.

**Active Line detail:** retain the same window and card groups after launch, adding a compact live summary and direct access to running/upcoming Trips. Separate lifecycle state, operational incidents and readiness of future proposals. Cards show current route, operating plan, vehicle/staff/facility coverage, rights/capacity and period-labelled results. All variants / a specific Pattern scopes the view without changing operation; an unlaunched variant is not a fault of the running Line. Keep cancelled/demand-driven journeys understandable, object links direct and estimates distinct from facts. Prepare change opens a separately labelled future proposal using the same cards; saving never applies it. Applying a version, suspending and closing use explicit scope, effective-time and consequence checks. Section 6.4 / UI-D12 owns this confirmed presentation, not new operating mechanics.

**Stations and terminals:** show an operational summary, directly accessible serving-Line information and relevant management cards, respecting ownership and information permissions. Open a separate movable station-style Arrivals/Departures board beside the station detail, with actual call times, destinations/origins, Lines, operators, dynamic platforms/stands and useful status. The Line list separately shows calling variants, directions, service calendars/frequency and next calls. Both reuse exact-object links and existing live state; drafts and pass-through routes are not advertised service. The embedded track/platform/stand schematic is deferred for now, without removing real station geometry or main-map interaction. See [UI_STATIONS.md](docs/UI_STATIONS.md), including UI-D14 and its acceptance scenarios.

**Depots, garages and workshops:** show actual operating, parking and maintenance roles, a compact activity/bottleneck overview and direct On site / Expected / Linked vehicle lists. Physical location, planned arrival and preferred facility are distinct. Six task-based cards cover preparation, repairs, supplies, capacity/equipment, staff and costs/agreements; supported Lines show the site's actual role with exact-object links. Routine work remains automated, shortages are explained, and rented-site controls respect existing rights. No embedded schematic or live camera is required. [UI_DEPOTS.md](docs/UI_DEPOTS.md) owns UI-D16 and its acceptance scenarios; it does not change physical service, resource commitments or dispatch rules.

**Commercial UI:** Business / Obchod contains Market, Opportunities, Offers and Contracts as distinct commercial views. Market exposes legitimately known local commodity supply/demand/reference-price intelligence and can project a selected commodity onto the main map; public tenders remain Opportunities with concrete service proposals, sealed rival bids and transparent published scoring. Opportunity feasibility is concise and explainable; offer drafts are persistent, editable in non-linear cards and never submitted implicitly. Contracts connect their binding terms directly to Transport Plans, Shipments, Lines, Trips, facilities and real fulfilment without conflating reservations with physical movement. Submission, amendment, renewal and termination remain explicit validated actions with visible consequences. [UI_COMMERCIAL.md](docs/UI_COMMERCIAL.md) owns UI-D17 and its acceptance scenarios.

**Construction UI:** Build opens compact floating catalogue/tools over the map. Save incomplete plans across sessions without starting works. Explicit land acquisition and accepted supply/contractor agreements remain real commitments even before launch. Start project revalidates the displayed scope, staged dependencies, costs and impact without buying prior commitments twice. The same window then shows physical work progress, current/next stage, supported completion estimates and linked blockers. A draft is not a reserved corridor or usable infrastructure; later changes cannot overwrite completed work. [UI_FLEET.md](docs/UI_FLEET.md) owns vehicle-order delivery presentation; [UI_CONSTRUCTION.md](docs/UI_CONSTRUCTION.md) owns UI-D18 and its acceptance scenarios.

**Finance UI:** Company / Finance and the upper-left HUD cash/current-period result open the same compact workspace. Show current cash, a clearly defined period result and upcoming commitments, with cards for revenues/costs, payments, loans, investments/plans and operating results. Significant figures drill into their actual sources while retaining reporting periods. Uncommitted scenarios, binding obligations and posted payments stay separate; shared costs, deposits and settlements are not counted twice. Loans affect cash and debt without becoming transport revenue. Forecast inspection never buys, borrows, pays or launches anything. [UI_FINANCE.md](docs/UI_FINANCE.md) owns UI-D19 and its acceptance scenarios, without adding a new accounting or banking simulation.

**Company, branches and workforce:** Company opens a compact scope-aware overview with cards for Branches, Workforce, Management and authority, Licences and permissions, and Company systems. Issues link directly to their causes; Finance reuses UI-D19. Branch details distinguish actual commercial coverage, premises, equipment, staffing, overload and director vacancy. Ordinary professions use shared company salary policies and aggregate recruitment, preserving company-wide mobile crews versus facility-bound staff. Named managers show skills separately from delegated scope, effective policies and decision history. Permissions and adopted systems do not create physical capacity or unlock unknown opportunities. [UI_COMPANY.md](docs/UI_COMPANY.md) owns UI-D20 and its acceptance scenarios without adding HR micromanagement or a mandatory organizational-chart editor.

**Map layers and opportunity discovery:** keep the normal map clean and provide purpose-based layers with one primary analysis, a legend, relevant filters and explicit Off. Maps must reveal where to grow as well as what to fix. Demand and opportunities distinguishes real discoverable jobs/tenders from evidence-backed potential: underserved connections, freight demand, compatible return-leg/spare-capacity use, growth/seasonality and investment to investigate. Markers and a compact linked list expose source, direction, period, uncertainty and the next existing planning action. No fabricated demand, guaranteed-profit score, private-data leak, double booking or automatic launch is allowed. [UI_MAP_LAYERS.md](docs/UI_MAP_LAYERS.md) owns UI-D21, confirming the earlier Section 8 map outline without changing economic or discovery rules.

**Shipment and split-cargo UI:** one Shipment remains the commercial item even when execution splits it into several CargoLots and Trips. Future reservations never look like physical movement, and a selected split portion retains its own location, readiness, deadline risk and links to Trips, vehicles, terminals and contracts. Routine allocation/recovery remains automated within canonical rules; exceptions are inspectable and manual changes cannot rewrite other portions or completed handling. **Trip detail:** one concrete dated Trip has a prominent stop/call progression with planned, actual and estimated times kept distinct, causal delay/recovery explanation, dynamic platform/stand state and segment-specific passenger/freight capacity. UI-D27's complete bilateral list retains active, future committed, pending and historical separation. Public/observed/contractual/commercial/estimated information keeps its source and time scope; old sightings are not current fleet totals, and private duties, bids, accounts and unrelated agreements remain hidden. Offers lead to canonical workflows, while renaming/acquisition/dissolution preserves identity and history. [UI_EXTERNAL_COMPANIES.md](docs/UI_EXTERNAL_COMPANIES.md) owns UI-D27/UI-D37. During manual pause, camera/inspection and all planning tools remain available, while the shared simulation clock and time-driven operations stay stopped. Closing a planner never releases the manual pause; resuming never silently submits a draft or catches up the real time spent paused. See UI-D04 and Section 9.1 of the UI document for the confirmed rule and its command-safety boundaries.

**Critical incidents:** automatic pause is enabled by default for critical events requiring prompt player attention, including while a planner is open. Ordinary delays and routine problems handled within authorized policies do not interrupt play. Show the cause, impact and available response; preserve planning and require an explicit player action to resume. Closing or acknowledging a notice does not restart time. Deduplicate unchanged incidents across repeated alerts and save/load; a new critical incident or material escalation may pause again. Section 7.2 and the critical-event part of UI-D08 own this rule and its event-boundary/persistence safeguards.

**Still implementation-level/open:** exact styling tokens/pixel dimensions, secondary-control placement, the exact presentation of contextual construction/planner tools, and per-screen layout details not explicitly locked by the focused specifications. Focus-loss behaviour, critical-event settings, pause-menu lifecycle, final HUD/navigation/search taxonomy and canonical CZ/EN terminology are already confirmed and must not be treated as pending design. Connection-agreement mechanics remain separate unresolved gameplay design in their dedicated specification. The focused UI acceptance scenarios describe evidence still to collect, not an implemented or tested UI.

## Wider base game and planned DLC

The wider base-game design has selectable new-game starts in **1900, 1925, 1950 and 1975**. Only the 1900 preset is required for the first playable target; it is not yet implemented in the inspected documentation-only baseline. Later presets initialize an appropriate existing world while the player still starts with a small company.

The earlier playable period, intended to begin around **1820**, is reserved for the first planned DLC, **Early Ages**. Historic buildings, steam operations, horse-drawn transport and suitable older vehicles can still be part of the base game where appropriate. The DLC release schedule is not specified.

## Shared time model

- A week has 7 days; a month has **14 days**; a year has **12 months / 168 days**.
- At **1x**, **one real second equals one game minute**.
- Running speeds: **0.5x, 1x, 2x, 4x, 8x and 16x**, plus pause.
- One common clock governs vehicles, operations, economics, construction, contracts, seasons and historical progression.

At uninterrupted 16x, reaching the reference year 2020 takes approximately **504 h from 1900**, **399 h from 1925**, **294 h from 1950**, or **189 h from 1975**. The later starts are wider-design comparisons, not first-release presets. These are mathematical durations assuming the selected simulation rate is sustained, not measured performance or promised completion times. **2020 is not a mandatory ending.**
